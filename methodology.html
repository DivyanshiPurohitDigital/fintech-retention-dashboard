Methodology breakdown

Fintech Customer Retention & Revenue Intelligence Dashboard · Simulated UK challenger bank dataset · 48,200 customers · 18-month observation window
Step 1
Cohort construction — SQL window function approach
Each cohort groups users by their first transaction month using DATE_TRUNC and MIN(txn_date). A FIRST_VALUE window function partitioned by cohort month calculates the retention percentage at each subsequent month relative to the cohort's starting size. Active is defined as at least one transaction recorded within the 30-day calendar window.
SELECT
  cohort_month,
  activity_month,
  COUNT(DISTINCT user_id) AS retained_users,
  ROUND(
    COUNT(DISTINCT user_id) * 100.0 /
    FIRST_VALUE(COUNT(DISTINCT user_id))
      OVER (
        PARTITION BY cohort_month
        ORDER BY activity_month
      ), 1
  ) AS retention_pct
FROM (
  SELECT
    user_id,
    DATE_TRUNC('month', MIN(txn_date)) AS cohort_month,
    DATE_TRUNC('month', txn_date)      AS activity_month
  FROM transactions
  GROUP BY user_id, DATE_TRUNC('month', txn_date)
) t
GROUP BY cohort_month, activity_month
ORDER BY cohort_month, activity_month;
Step 2
LTV segmentation — revenue-based bucketing
Users are assigned to segments based on 12-month cumulative revenue. Thresholds are calibrated against the simulated product tier structure of a UK challenger bank offering free, standard, and premium account types. Churn rate per segment is the average monthly churn across the observation window, calculated as users lost in month N divided by users active in month N-1.

Step 3
Churn-adjusted revenue forecast — quantifying £ at risk
The forecast line assumes current average revenue per user (ARPU) held constant with zero incremental churn from the observation baseline. The gap between forecast and actual revenue in each month represents revenue destroyed by churn. This reframes churn from an operational percentage into a direct £ business cost, making it actionable for product and commercial teams rather than remaining an abstract retention metric.
-- Revenue at risk per month
SELECT
  month,
  SUM(forecast_revenue - actual_revenue) AS revenue_at_risk
FROM (
  SELECT
    month,
    retained_users * avg_arpu        AS actual_revenue,
    baseline_users * avg_arpu        AS forecast_revenue
  FROM monthly_cohort_summary
) t
GROUP BY month
ORDER BY month;
Step 4
Key findings derivation — month-3 drop and activation split
The month-3 retention drop (-12pp) was identified by calculating the month-on-month delta across all four cohorts and averaging. It consistently represented the largest single-month churn event, indicating a structural onboarding friction point rather than a cohort-specific anomaly. The premium activation finding (91% vs 58% six-month retention) was derived by splitting the premium cohort at day 30 into two groups — users who activated three or more product features versus users who activated one — then comparing survival rates at month 6 using a basic retention query partitioned by the activation flag.
