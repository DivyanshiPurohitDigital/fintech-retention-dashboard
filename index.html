How this was built
Step 1 — Cohort construction (SQL)
Each cohort groups users by their first transaction month. A window function calculates how many users from that cohort remained active in each subsequent month. Active is defined as at least one transaction in the 30-day window.
SELECT
  cohort_month,
  activity_month,
  COUNT(DISTINCT user_id) AS retained_users,
  ROUND(COUNT(DISTINCT user_id) * 100.0 /
    FIRST_VALUE(COUNT(DISTINCT user_id))
    OVER (PARTITION BY cohort_month
          ORDER BY activity_month), 1) AS retention_pct
FROM (
  SELECT
    user_id,
    DATE_TRUNC('month', MIN(txn_date)) AS cohort_month,
    DATE_TRUNC('month', txn_date)      AS activity_month
  FROM transactions
  GROUP BY user_id, DATE_TRUNC('month', txn_date)
) t
GROUP BY cohort_month, activity_month
ORDER BY cohort_month, activity_month;
Step 2 — LTV segmentation logic
Users are segmented by 12-month cumulative revenue. Premium: £200+. Standard: £50–£199. Freemium: below £50. Churn rate per segment is calculated as users lost in month N divided by users active in month N-1, averaged across the observation window.
Step 3 — Churn-adjusted revenue forecast
Forecast revenue assumes current ARPU held constant with zero incremental churn. The gap between forecast and actual is the revenue destroyed by churn each month. This quantifies churn in £ terms rather than as a percentage, making it directly actionable for product and commercial teams.
Step 4 — Key findings derivation
The month-3 drop (-12pp) was identified by comparing month-on-month delta across all cohorts. The premium activation finding (91% vs 58% retention) came from splitting the premium cohort by feature adoption count at day 30 and comparing 6-month survival rates between the two groups.
